## Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive methodological review of the Satisfaction Solution Research Institute (SSRI), dedicated to partner-matching decision coaching for hard-tech commercialization entrepreneurs. We synthesize the Institute's theoretical foundations (satisficing theory, enterprise Confucianism, cognitive neuroscience), methodological innovations (Five-Dimension Decision Framework, 12-type conflict typology, automated assessment systems), and practical implementations (33-role AI Agent system, Token Economics governance, disaster recovery protocols). Drawing on 22 years of the founder's dual-system experience (banking + entrepreneurship), we demonstrate how academic theory translates into actionable decision support tools. We conclude with limitations, validation needs, and future research directions.

**Keywords**: Satisfaction Solution Research Institute; Hard-tech; Partner matching; Decision coaching; Methodological review

## 1. Introduction

Hard-tech commercialization — transforming laboratory innovations into market-ready products — requires long cycles (5-10 years), large capital ($10M-$100M+), cross-disciplinary teams, and complex partnerships. In this environment, the partnership between technical founder and commercialization partner is often decisive.

The Satisfaction Solution Research Institute was founded to address the gap between partner selection as "art" and the need for systematic "science." This paper reviews the complete methodological system.

## 2. Theoretical Foundations

### 2.1 Satisficing Theory (Herbert A. Simon)

Core philosophy based on Simon (1955):
- **Bounded rationality**: Decision-makers face information, cognitive, and time constraints;
- **Satisficing**: Set aspiration levels, stop searching when met;
- **Procedural rationality**: Process quality matters more than outcomes.

We do not help entrepreneurs find "perfect" partners; we help them find "good enough" partners through rational processes.

### 2.2 Enterprise Confucianism (Li Honglei)

Ethical framework from Professor Li Honglei's enterprise Confucianism (Li, 2017, 2023):
- Three principles: Unity of heaven-humanity, virtue-ritual, righteousness-profit;
- Five constants for partnerships: Ren, Yi, Li, Zhi, Xin;
- Six virtues for partners: Character, righteousness, propriety, wisdom, trust, courage.

### 2.3 Cognitive Neuroscience

Intuition training grounded in neuroscience:
- Neuroplasticity: Business intuition is trainable;
- Dual-system theory: System 1 and System 2 optimization;
- Neurofeedback: BCI-based pattern recognition and stress management enhancement.

## 3. Methodological Innovations

### 3.1 Five-Dimension Decision Framework (5DDF)

| Dimension | Totem | Element | Core Question |
|-----------|-------|---------|---------------|
| Character Foundation | LIU | Earth | Is this person worth deep conversation? |
| Methodological Core | SIMON | Metal | Does this meet satisficing standards? |
| State & Insight | GUANYIN | Water | Can I maintain clarity under pressure? |
| Ethical Governance | CONFUCIUS | Wood | Does this align with ren-yi-li-zhi-xin? |
| Intuitive Action | HUINENG | Fire | Is my intuition trained or wild? |

### 3.2 Twelve-Type Conflict Typology

Diagnostic framework mapping 12 conflict types to Five Elements, with prevention strategies for each.

### 3.3 Automated Assessment System (PDMAS)

Five-dimension maturity scoring with automated report generation and case library matching.

### 3.4 AI Agent Decision Support System

33-role system providing 24/7 multi-perspective decision support through spiritual totems, expert avatars, and AI-native operational roles.

## 6. Limitations and Future Directions

### 6.1 Limitations

1. 12-type case library and PDMAS require larger-scale validation;
2. 5DDF is grounded in Chinese culture; cross-cultural applicability needs testing;
3. Long-term partnership outcomes associated with methodology need tracking.

### 6.2 Future Research

1. Large-scale statistical validation of partner matching outcomes;
2. Cross-cultural adaptation testing;
3. Real-time BCI monitoring during partner negotiations;
4. LLM-based partner matching with retrieval-augmented generation;
5. Formal certification programs for partner-matching decision coaches.

---

## 7. Conclusion

The Satisfaction Solution Research Institute represents a systematic attempt to transform partner-matching from art to "scientific art" — honoring both analytical rigor and human dimensions of trust, intuition, and ethics.

The core insight: partner matching is not about finding the "best" person but the "right" person through a "good enough" process. This insight, rooted in Simon's satisficing theory, is simultaneously humbling and empowering.

As the founder reflects: "I have seen too many 'wrongs' to know what is 'right.'" The methodology is an attempt to share that hard-won wisdom.

The journey from "blowing sand to gold" continues.
