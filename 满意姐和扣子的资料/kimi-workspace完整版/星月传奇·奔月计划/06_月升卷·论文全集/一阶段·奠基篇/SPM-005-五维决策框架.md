## Abstract

This paper proposes the "Five-Dimension Decision Framework" (5DDF), an original decision-making model that integrates five Chinese cultural totems with Western decision science. The five dimensions — LIU (Earth/Character), SIMON (Metal/Methodology), GUANYIN (Water/State), CONFUCIUS (Wood/Ethics), and HUINENG (Fire/Intuition) — form a complete decision cycle that addresses the cognitive, ethical, methodological, emotional, and action-oriented aspects of decision-making. Drawing on Herbert Simon's satisficing theory, Confucian ethics, Zen Buddhism, and cognitive neuroscience, the 5DDF offers a holistic alternative to purely rational or purely intuitive models. The framework has been operationalized through a 33-role AI Agent system and validated through case studies in entrepreneurial partner matching.

**Keywords**: Five dimensions; Decision framework; Chinese culture; Western science; Satisficing

## 1. Introduction

Decision-making frameworks in Western management science have traditionally emphasized rational analysis — cost-benefit analysis, decision trees, game theory. While powerful, these tools often neglect the emotional, ethical, and intuitive dimensions that profoundly influence real-world decisions (Kahneman, 2011).

Conversely, Eastern philosophies — Confucianism, Buddhism, Taoism — offer rich insights into the human dimensions of decision-making but often lack systematic, operationalizable frameworks.

This paper bridges this gap by proposing the Five-Dimension Decision Framework (5DDF), which integrates five Chinese cultural-spiritual archetypes with rigorous decision science. The framework is not merely a cultural metaphor; it is a functional decision architecture that has been operationalized and tested in a real-world AI Agent system.

## 2. Theoretical Foundation

### 2.1 The Five Elements (Wu Xing) as a Systemic Model

The Chinese Five Elements (Wu Xing: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water) is not a primitive chemistry but a sophisticated systems theory describing dynamic interactions and transformations. Each element:
- Has a generating ("mother") relationship with one element;
- Has a controlling ("grandparent") relationship with another;
- Exists in a perpetual cycle of creation and transformation.

This systems-thinking approach is remarkably compatible with modern complexity science and network theory (Capra, 1996).

### 2.2 The Five Totems

The 5DDF maps five historical-cultural figures to the Five Elements, each representing a decision dimension:

| Totem | Element | Dimension | Core Question | Western Parallel |
|-------|---------|-----------|---------------|----------------|
| **LIU** | Earth | Character Foundation | Is this person worth deep conversation? | Ethical leadership |
| **SIMON** | Metal | Methodological Core | Does this decision meet satisficing standards? | Bounded rationality |
| **GUANYIN** | Water | State & Insight | Can I maintain clarity under pressure? | Mindfulness |
| **CONFUCIUS** | Wood | Ethical Governance | Does this align with ren-yi-li-zhi-xin? | Virtue ethics |
| **HUINENG** | Fire | Intuitive Action | Is my intuition trained or wild? | System 1 thinking |

### 2.3 Generating Cycle

The five dimensions follow a generating cycle:

```
LIU (Earth) → SIMON (Metal) → GUANYIN (Water) → CONFUCIUS (Wood) → HUINENG (Fire)
Character   Methodology      State/Insight      Ethics             Intuition/Action
```

This means:
- Character (LIU) grounds methodology (SIMON);
- Methodology (SIMON) generates clarity (GUANYIN);
- Clarity (GUANYIN) nourishes ethical growth (CONFUCIUS);
- Ethics (CONFUCIUS) fuels intuitive action (HUINENG);
- Action (HUINENG) produces results that feed back into character development (LIU).

## 3. Framework Operationalization

### 3.1 The 5DDF Decision Process

For any major decision (e.g., partner selection), the decision-maker proceeds through five steps:

**Step 1: LIU Assessment — Character Foundation**
- Evaluate the candidate's ethical foundation;
- Check: Is this person "worth deep conversation"?
- Tool: Ethical background check, peer references.

**Step 2: SIMON Assessment — Methodological Fit**
- Apply satisficing criteria;
- Check: Does the candidate meet minimum thresholds across all dimensions?
- Tool: Satisficing Partner Matching Model (SPMM).

**Step 3: GUANYIN Assessment — State Calibration**
- Evaluate the candidate's mental stability under pressure;
- Check: How does this person behave in crisis?
- Tool: Stress-testing scenarios, reference checks about past crises.

**Step 4: CONFUCIUS Assessment — Ethical Alignment**
- Evaluate values alignment using the Five Constants;
- Check: Do our ethical foundations match?
- Tool: Values alignment interview.

**Step 5: HUINENG Assessment — Intuitive Calibration**
- Test business intuition;
- Check: Is this person's intuition trained or just guessing?
- Tool: Business case simulations, pattern-recognition tests.

## 4. Case Study: Partner Selection for a Hard-Tech Startup

### 4.1 Case Background

A semiconductor startup ("ChipVision") needed a co-founder with commercialization expertise. Three candidates were evaluated using the 5DDF.

### 4.2 Evaluation Results

| Dimension | Candidate A | Candidate B | Candidate C |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| **LIU (Character)** | 9/10 | 7/10 | 8/10 |
| **SIMON (Method)** | 6/10 | 9/10 | 7/10 |
| **GUANYIN (State)** | 7/10 | 8/10 | 6/10 |
| **CONFUCIUS (Ethics)** | 8/10 | 6/10 | 9/10 |
| **HUINENG (Intuition)** | 7/10 | 7/10 | 8/10 |
| **Total** | 37/50 | 37/50 | 38/50 |

### 4.3 Decision

While Candidate C had the highest total score, the 5DDF analysis revealed that Candidate A excelled in Character (LIU) — the foundational dimension. Candidate B excelled in Methodology (SIMON) but was weak in Ethics (CONFUCIUS). Candidate C was balanced but lacked standout strengths.

The final decision: Select Candidate A, with a plan to strengthen methodological training (SIMON dimension) through mentorship.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Theoretical Contribution

The 5DDF makes three contributions:
1. **Integration**: It bridges Eastern wisdom traditions with Western decision science;
2. **Operationalization**: It transforms philosophical concepts into measurable, actionable dimensions;
3. **Holism**: It addresses the full spectrum of human decision-making — rational, emotional, ethical, and intuitive.

### 5.2 Comparison with Existing Frameworks

| Framework | Dimensions | Culture | Operationalization |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| SWOT | 4 | Western | High |
| PESTEL | 6 | Western | High |
| Porter's Five Forces | 5 | Western | High |
| OODA Loop | 4 | Western | Medium |
| **5DDF** | **5** | **East-West Fusion** | **High** |

### 5.3 Limitations

1. The framework's effectiveness depends on the assessor's cultural understanding;
2. Weight assignment across dimensions may vary by context;
3. The framework needs larger-scale empirical validation.

---

## 6. Conclusion

The Five-Dimension Decision Framework offers a holistic, culturally-grounded approach to decision-making. By integrating five Chinese cultural totems with Western decision science, it addresses the limitations of purely rational models while maintaining rigor and operationalizability.

In an era of increasing complexity and uncertainty, decision-makers need tools that honor both the analytical and the human dimensions of choice. The 5DDF is one such tool — a bridge between ancient wisdom and modern science, between East and West, between analysis and intuition.
