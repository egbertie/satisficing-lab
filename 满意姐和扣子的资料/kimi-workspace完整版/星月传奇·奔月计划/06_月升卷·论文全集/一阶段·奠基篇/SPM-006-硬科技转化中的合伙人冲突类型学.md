## Abstract

Partner conflicts are a leading cause of startup failure, particularly in hard-tech sectors where long development cycles and high capital requirements amplify tensions. This paper presents a 12-type typology of partner conflicts, constructed through grounded theory analysis of real-world cases and theoretical deduction. The typology maps each conflict type to the Five-Element decision framework, providing both diagnosis and treatment tools. Each type includes: definition, identification guide, typical case analysis, five-dimension analysis, prevention strategy, repair protocol, tool templates, and expert consultation records. While the case library (V1.0, 84 documents) requires classroom testing for refinement, it represents a systematic attempt to transform scattered entrepreneurial experience into reusable knowledge.

**Keywords**: Partner conflict; Hard-tech; Typology; Case library; Conflict resolution

---

## 1. Introduction

Hard-tech commercialization — transforming laboratory innovations into market-ready products — is a high-stakes, high-risk endeavor. The process typically spans 5-10 years, requires $10M-$100M in capital, and faces technical, market, and regulatory uncertainties (Blank, 2020). In this environment, the partnership between the technical founder and the commercialization partner is often the decisive factor.

Yet partner conflicts are endemic. Studies suggest that 65% of startup failures can be traced to partnership problems (Noce, 2020). In hard-tech specifically, the problem is worse: technical founders and business partners often speak different "languages," operate on different timelines, and have different risk appetites.

This paper addresses this gap by proposing a systematic typology of partner conflicts, constructed from real-world observation and theoretical analysis.

## 2. Methodology

### 2.1 Data Sources

The typology is built on three data sources:
1. **Founder's experience**: Egbertie's 22 years of dual-system experience (banking + entrepreneurship);
2. **Industry cases**: Cases from entrepreneurship education platforms (e.g., Yitang);
3. **Theoretical deduction**: Extension of established conflict typologies to the partner context.

### 2.2 Typology Construction

The typology was constructed using a grounded theory approach:
1. **Open coding**: Initial categorization of observed conflict patterns;
2. **Axial coding**: Identification of relationships between categories;
3. **Selective coding**: Integration around a core category (the Five-Element framework);
4. **Validation**: Cross-checking with theoretical literature and expert consultation.

## 3. The 12-Type Typology

### 3.1 Overview

| Type | Name | Core Conflict | Element |
|------|------|---------------|---------|
| T01 | Needs Mismatch | Partner skills don't match founder needs | Earth |
| T02 | Solution Disagreement | Divergent views on strategic direction | Metal |
| T03 | Cognitive Bias | Misaligned perceptions of contributions | Water |
| T04 | Ethical Violation | Breach of ren-yi-li-zhi-xin | Wood |
| T05 | Interest Imbalance | Unfair equity/profit distribution | Metal |
| T06 | Communication Breakdown | Failed information exchange mechanisms | Water |
| T07 | Excessive Pickiness | Perpetual search for "better" partners | Metal |
| T08 | Trust Collapse | Destruction of trust foundation | Wood |
| T09 | No Exit Path | Missing or unfair exit mechanisms | Fire |
| T10 | Role Ambiguity | Unclear division of responsibilities | Earth |
| T11 | Growth Asynchrony | Partners develop at different speeds | Fire |
| T12 | External Shock | External changes destroy partnership | Water |

### 3.2 Detailed Analysis of Selected Types

**Type 01: Needs Mismatch (Earth Dimension)**
- **Definition**: The founder needs a partner with skill set X, but recruits someone with skill set Y;
- **Typical Case**: A hardware founder recruits a software engineer as "co-founder," expecting them to handle business development;
- **Prevention**: Use SPMM's skill-complement dimension to clearly define needs before searching;
- **Repair**: Honest conversation about mismatched expectations; either redefining roles or graceful separation.

**Type 07: Excessive Pickiness (Metal Dimension)**
- **Definition**: Perpetual search for the "perfect" partner, never committing;
- **Typical Case**: Founder interviews 50+ candidates over 18 months, always finding a flaw; project dies from delay;
- **Root Cause**: Maximization bias — inability to accept "good enough";
- **Prevention**: Apply satisficing threshold; set hard time limits;
- **Repair**: Cognitive reframing — "perfect is the enemy of good."

**Type 08: Trust Collapse (Wood Dimension)**
- **Definition**: A single breach of trust destroys the partnership's ethical foundation;
- **Typical Case**: CEO secretly files core technology patents under personal name; CTO and COO quit;
- **Root Cause**: Over-reliance on legal contracts without ethical foundation;
- **Prevention**: Build "Five Constants" ethical consensus before legal agreements;
- **Repair**: Trust repair is extremely difficult; often requires third-party mediation or separation.

## 4. Five-Element Mapping

Each conflict type maps to one of the Five Elements, providing a diagnostic framework:

| Element | Conflict Types | Prevention Strategy |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Earth** | T01, T10 | Strengthen character assessment; clarify roles |
| **Metal** | T02, T05, T07 | Apply satisficing; balance interests early |
| **Water** | T03, T06, T12 | Build communication protocols; maintain state clarity |
| **Wood** | T04, T08 | Establish ethical consensus; build trust mechanisms |
| **Fire** | T09, T11 | Design exit mechanisms; align growth expectations |

## 5. Case Library Structure

Each type in the case library contains seven standard documents:
1. **Type Definition & Identification Guide**: How to recognize this conflict;
2. **Typical Case Deep Analysis**: Real-world (or realistic) case study;
3. **Five-Dimension Analysis**: Analysis through the 5DDF lens;
4. **Prevention Strategy**: How to avoid this conflict;
5. **Repair Protocol**: How to fix it if it occurs;
6. **Tool Templates**: Checklists, assessment forms, conversation guides;
7. **Expert Consultation Records**: Input from relevant expert avatars.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Theoretical Contribution

This paper contributes to the entrepreneurship literature by:
1. Providing the first systematic typology of partner conflicts in hard-tech;
2. Linking conflict types to a culturally-grounded decision framework (5DDF);
3. Offering both diagnostic and treatment tools for each type.

### 6.2 Limitations

1. The case library (V1.0) is based on founder observation and theoretical deduction; classroom testing is needed for validation;
2. The 12-type framework may not cover all conflict patterns; future research may identify additional types;
3. Cultural applicability outside the Chinese context needs testing.

---

## 7. Conclusion

Partner conflicts in hard-tech commercialization are not random events — they follow predictable patterns. The 12-type typology provides entrepreneurs with a diagnostic framework: when conflict arises, identify the type, understand its root cause, and apply the corresponding prevention or repair strategy.

The case library transforms scattered entrepreneurial experience into systematic, reusable knowledge. While it requires further validation, it represents a step toward making entrepreneurship education more evidence-based and systematic.

As the founder's experience shows: "I have seen too many 'wrongs' to know what is 'right.'" The case library is an attempt to share that hard-won wisdom.
