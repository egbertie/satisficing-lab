## Abstract

Partnership agreements, like software systems, often fail not because of expected conditions but because of edge cases and adversarial scenarios. This paper adapts AI "red-teaming" methodology — systematic adversarial testing designed to find failure modes — to partnership agreement design. Drawing on adversarial testing practices from the AI industry (particularly DJI's AI Blue Army approach), the paper proposes a five-dimensional stress testing framework for partnership agreements: interest conflict, power struggle, exit scenarios, external shocks, and long-term alignment. The framework provides entrepreneurs with a systematic tool to identify and mitigate partnership vulnerabilities before they become crises.

**Keywords**: AI red-teaming; Stress testing; Partnership agreements; Adversarial testing; Risk management

## 1. Introduction

AI systems are tested not by asking "Does it work?" but by asking "How can we make it fail?" This adversarial mindset — known as "red-teaming" — has become standard practice in AI safety (Ganguli et al., 2022). The same mindset is rarely applied to partnership agreements, yet the consequences of partnership failure can be far more damaging than software failure.

This paper proposes adapting AI red-teaming methodology to partnership agreement design. The core insight: partnership agreements should be stress-tested against adversarial scenarios before signing, not evaluated against ideal conditions.

---

## 2. AI Red-Teaming: Core Principles

### 2.1 What is Red-Teaming?

Red-teaming is "the practice of rigorously challenging plans, policies, systems, and assumptions by adopting an adversarial approach" (UK Cabinet Office, 2013). In AI, it involves:
- **Adversarial input generation**: Creating inputs designed to confuse or break the system;
- **Failure mode enumeration**: Systematically identifying all possible failure modes;
- **Stress testing**: Pushing the system to its limits;
- **Boundary exploration**: Defining the system's safe operating envelope.

### 2.2 Why Red-Teaming Applies to Partnerships

Partnerships and AI systems share structural similarities:
- Both are complex systems with multiple interacting components;
- Both operate under uncertainty and incomplete information;
- Both can fail catastrophically under edge-case conditions;
- Both benefit from proactive failure mode identification.

## 3. The Five-Dimensional Stress Testing Framework

### 3.1 Dimension 1: Interest Conflict

**Stress Test Question**: "When company valuation drops from $300M to $30M, what does each partner choose?"

**Adversarial Scenarios**:
- Company misses revenue target by 50%;
- Key patent is invalidated;
- Major customer cancels contract;
- Funding round fails at the last minute.

**Agreement Stress Points**:
- Equity split: Does it reflect actual contribution under stress?
- Vesting schedules: Do they incentivize staying or encourage leaving?
- Anti-dilution: Do protections become weapons under stress?

### 3.2 Dimension 2: Power Struggle

**Stress Test Question**: "When founder and partner disagree on a critical strategic decision, who decides?"

**Adversarial Scenarios**:
- Founder wants to pivot; partner wants to stay the course;
- Partner wants to raise more capital; founder wants to preserve equity;
- Disagreement on CEO replacement;
- Disagreement on acquisition offer.

**Agreement Stress Points**:
- Decision-making authority: Is it clear who decides what?
- Tie-breaking mechanisms: What happens when votes are split?
- Veto rights: Do they prevent deadlock or create it?

### 3.3 Dimension 3: Exit Scenarios

**Stress Test Question**: "When a partner wants to leave, is the exit mechanism clear, fair, and executable?"

**Adversarial Scenarios**:
- Partner quits during critical funding round;
- Partner is terminated for cause;
- Partner dies or becomes incapacitated;
- Partner wants to start a competing business.

**Agreement Stress Points**:
- Buy-sell provisions: Is valuation formula fair under stress?
- Non-compete: Is it enforceable and reasonable?
- IP ownership: Who owns work in progress?
- Client relationships: Can departing partner poach clients?

### 3.4 Dimension 4: External Shocks

**Stress Test Question**: "When an external shock hits (pandemic, regulatory change, competitor breakthrough), does the partnership hold or fracture?"

**Adversarial Scenarios**:
- New regulation bans core business model;
- Competitor launches superior product at 50% price;
- Key supplier goes bankrupt;
- Global pandemic disrupts supply chain.

**Agreement Stress Points**:
- Force majeure: Is it defined broadly enough?
- Emergency decision-making: Can partners act quickly without full consensus?
- Cost-cutting authority: Who decides layoffs and budget cuts?

### 3.5 Dimension 5: Long-Term Alignment

**Stress Test Question**: "In 5 years, will the partners still want the same things?"

**Adversarial Scenarios**:
- One partner wants IPO; other wants acquisition;
- One partner wants to expand internationally; other wants to focus domestically;
- One partner wants to bring in new investors; other wants to maintain control;
- Life changes: marriage, children, health issues.

**Agreement Stress Points**:
- Long-term vision alignment: Is there a mechanism for regular realignment?
- Succession planning: What if a partner can no longer perform?
- Lifestyle expectations: Do partners have compatible work-life balance goals?

## 4. Stress Testing Protocol

### 4.1 Testing Process

The red-teaming process for partnership agreements follows five steps:

```
1. Agreement Review → 2. Scenario Generation → 3. Stress Application → 4. Failure Analysis → 5. Remediation
```

**Step 1: Agreement Review**
- Legal team reviews all agreement provisions;
- Identify ambiguities, gaps, and potential conflict points.

**Step 2: Scenario Generation**
- Generate 20-30 adversarial scenarios across five dimensions;
- Include both likely and "black swan" scenarios.

**Step 3: Stress Application**
- For each scenario, simulate how the agreement would perform;
- Identify points where the agreement breaks, creates perverse incentives, or produces ambiguous outcomes.

**Step 4: Failure Analysis**
- Classify failures by severity (critical/major/minor);
- Prioritize remediation based on likelihood and impact.

**Step 5: Remediation**
- Rewrite provisions to address identified failure modes;
- Add contingency clauses for high-impact scenarios;
- Test remediated agreement with new scenarios.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Theoretical Contribution

This paper contributes to:
1. AI safety: Extending red-teaming from software to social systems;
2. Contract design: Introducing adversarial testing to partnership agreements;
3. Risk management: Providing a systematic framework for partnership risk identification.

### 5.2 Practical Implications

For entrepreneurs:
- **Don't sign without stress testing**: Partnership agreements should be red-teamed before signing;
- **Invest in scenario planning**: The scenarios generated during red-teaming become valuable strategic planning tools;
- **Update regularly**: Stress test annually or when major changes occur.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Partnership agreements should be treated like critical software systems — tested not for ideal conditions but for edge cases and adversarial scenarios. The five-dimensional stress testing framework provides a systematic approach to identifying partnership vulnerabilities before they become crises.

The red-teaming mindset — "How can we make this fail?" — is uncomfortable but essential. It is far better to discover a fatal flaw in the agreement during stress testing than to discover it during a real crisis.

As the AI safety community has learned: "The question is not whether the system will fail, but when and how." The same applies to partnerships.
