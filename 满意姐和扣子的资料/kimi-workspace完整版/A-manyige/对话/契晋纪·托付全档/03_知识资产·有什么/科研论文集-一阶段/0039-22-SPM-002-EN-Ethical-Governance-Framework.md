## Abstract

Partnership relationships are among the most central yet fragile elements of modern enterprise governance. This paper, based on Professor Li Honglei's "enterprise Confucianism" theory, constructs an ethical governance framework for partnerships. The framework transforms the Confucian "Five Constants" (Ren-Yi-Li-Zhi-Xin) into five governance dimensions for partnership relationships: Ren — care and altruism; Yi — righteous profit distribution; Li — collaborative norms and procedures; Zhi — complementary wisdom; Xin — trust foundation. Through theoretical analysis and case studies, we demonstrate that enterprise Confucianism is not a modern revival of "ancient moral dogma" but an operational, cross-culturally applicable partnership governance toolkit. We also propose the "Six-Virtue Partner" assessment model, providing entrepreneurs with Confucian ethics-based partner screening criteria.

**Keywords**: Enterprise Confucianism; Partnership ethics; Confucian business; Five constants; Trust governance

## 1. Introduction

Partnership dissolution is a leading cause of business failure (Noce, 2020). In hard-tech commercialization — where technical barriers, capital requirements, and development cycles are substantial — partnership trust and collaboration quality directly determine project survival.

Traditional partnership governance frameworks over-rely on legal contracts (equity agreements, non-competes, exit mechanisms), neglecting ethical construction. Law is the floor; ethics is the ceiling — without an ethical foundation, even the most comprehensive legal documents cannot sustain long-term partnerships.

This paper advances a core proposition: the Confucian "Five Constants" (Ren, Yi, Li, Zhi, Xin) can provide a systematic ethical governance framework for modern partnerships. This proposition builds upon Professor Li Honglei's three-decade enterprise Confucianism research (Li, 2017, 2023).

## 2. Theoretical Foundation: Enterprise Confucianism

### 2.1 Definition and Positioning

Professor Li defines enterprise Confucianism as: "In disciplinary positioning, enterprise Confucianism is a new domain of contemporary Confucianism and a Chinese expression of contemporary enterprise theory; in theoretical form, it represents the creative transformation of millennium-old Confucian tradition and innovative development of century-old enterprise theory; in practical foundation, it summarizes and distills excellent traditional Chinese culture integrated into modern enterprise management practice — the crystallization of new Confucian entrepreneur governance wisdom" (Li, 2023).

Enterprise Confucianism transcends Western management theory's narrow focus on "management" (techniques for solving operational problems), emphasizing "governance" (seeking long-term stability and development). This "Dao-technique integration" governance wisdom offers particular insights for partnership relationships.

### 2.2 Three Fundamental Principles

**Principle 1: Unity of Heaven and Humanity**
- Reverence for heavenly principle as faith foundation;
- Filial piety and ancestor respect as faith basis;
- Care for people and cherish things as faith practice.

**Principle 2: Unity of Virtue and Ritual**
- Moral education as humanistic cultivation;
- Ritual norms as institutional standards;
- Dao-governing-technique as operational method.

**Principle 3: Unity of Righteousness and Profit**
- Righteousness-driven profit as business goal;
- Self-cultivation and world-benefit as business path;
- Integrity-based business ethics.

## 3. Five Constants Ethics and Partnership Governance

### 3.1 From "Five Constants" to "Five-Dimension Partnership Governance"

| Confucian Ethic | Partnership Governance Dimension | Core Question | Governance Tool |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Ren (Benevolence)** | Care Dimension | Does the partner care about mutual growth? | Regular one-on-one deep conversations |
| **Yi (Righteousness)** | Profit Dimension | Is profit distribution fair and righteous? | Dynamic equity adjustment mechanism |
| **Li (Propriety)** | Procedure Dimension | Are collaboration norms clear? | Partnership议事规则 |
| **Zhi (Wisdom)** | Complementarity Dimension | Are partners intellectually complementary? | Capability matrix assessment |
| **Xin (Trust)** | Trust Dimension | Is trust firmly established? | Trust scoring system |

### 3.2 The "Six-Virtue Partner" Assessment Model

Building on enterprise Confucianism, we propose a "Six-Virtue Partner" model:

| Dimension | Confucian Ethic | Assessment Standard | Weight |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------------|--------|
| Character Foundation | Ren | Care for others, altruistic spirit | 20% |
| Righteous Profit | Yi | Unity of righteousness and profit, not profit-obsessed | 15% |
| Norm Consciousness | Li | Rule compliance, procedural justice | 15% |
| Wisdom Complementarity | Zhi | Capability complementarity, cognitive diversity | 20% |
| Integrity Record | Xin | Promise fulfillment, consistency | 20% |
| Courage to Shoulder | Yong | Standing up in crisis | 10% |

"Yong" (courage) as the sixth dimension comes from Li's "courage to shoulder responsibility" (Li, 2023). In partnerships, courage manifests as: not逃避ing difficulties, admitting mistakes, leading change.

## 4. Case Study: Trust Collapse Failure (Type 08)

A biotech company founded by three partners (CEO — technical background, CTO — academic background, COO — business background) had detailed equity agreements and non-compete clauses. After Series A funding, the CTO discovered the CEO had personally filed patents for core technology without partnership meeting discussion. Though legally ambiguous, this action destroyed trust. The CTO and COO departed; the company collapsed.

**Analysis**: Legal contracts cannot substitute for ethical trust. If the three partners had established a "Five Constants" ethical consensus — especially Xin (trust) and Yi (righteous profit) — the CEO might not have made this shortsighted decision.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Cross-Cultural Applicability

The "Five Constants" possess cross-cultural universality:
- "Ren" aligns with Western "servant leadership";
- "Yi" connects with Western "distributive justice";
- "Li" resembles Western "organizational routines";
- "Zhi" matches Western "cognitive diversity";
- "Xin" is the foundation of business relationships across all cultures.

### 5.2 Limitations

1. Six-Virtue weight assignment requires larger-scale empirical validation;
2. Enterprise Confucianism's partnership governance effects need longitudinal study;
3. Cultural expression differences across regions require attention.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Partnership relationships are not merely legal contractual relationships but ethical covenant relationships. Law is the floor; ethics is the ceiling. Based on Professor Li Honglei's enterprise Confucianism theory, this paper constructs a "Five-Dimension Partnership Governance" framework and "Six-Virtue Partner" assessment model.

Enterprise Confucianism is not "revival" but "creative transformation." Transforming millennium-old Confucian wisdom into modern enterprise governance tools honors tradition while contributing to contemporary business civilization.

The highest partnership governance is not "prevention" (legal constraints) but "transformation" (ethical cultivation). When partners' internal ethics align with external norms, partnerships can truly achieve long-term stability.
